01
Production Operations Team Leadership SLA & Escalations

Leading High-Volume Production Shifts for International Clients

Context

As Team Leader of a high-end photo production team, I ran daily editing and retouching workflows for real-estate clients mainly in the US, Australia and Europe — with around 60 in-office and remote team members per shift, working alongside colleagues in Hanoi and at the company's headquarters in Denmark.

Challenge

Every shift mixed routine orders with rush requests, last-minute changes and unclear client instructions — all against SLA, quality and delivery deadlines. One late decision could hold up the whole queue.

What I Did
  • Opened each shift by reviewing workload, the previous shift's handover, urgent tasks and email to set priorities
  • Allocated work in real time by capacity, skill, complexity, priority and deadline — routing difficult or urgent orders to in-office editors and well-defined orders to remote staff to protect quality
  • Monitored progress and capacity through Citrix, Power BI and internal dashboards, and reallocated resources when bottlenecks appeared
  • Gave the Customer Success team realistic ETAs and clear explanations so they could set client expectations, and clarified unclear requirements with clients by email
  • Handled escalations and stakeholder updates, keeping ownership until each issue was resolved or escalated
  • Standardised SOPs and shift procedures, coached team members and followed up on performance
When things broke

When the internal system failed in the middle of urgent jobs, I moved team communication to an external tool, had editors continue offline, delivered files through cloud storage and used quick calls to avoid misunderstandings — so urgent orders kept moving.

✓ Improved service consistency and client satisfaction
✓ Realistic ETAs that let Customer Success set clear client expectations
✓ Urgent deliveries kept moving through an internal system outage
✓ Standardised SOPs and clear cross-shift handovers

04
AI & Automation Make.com Research Ops

AI-Assisted Research Automation

Context

A freelance project required tracking fire-safety (PCCC) information across Vietnam, Singapore, Southeast Asia and international markets. Collecting and summarising updates by hand was slow and hard to keep consistent.

Challenge

The project needed a steady, structured feed of relevant updates — not a pile of links — without hours of manual reading and copy-pasting.

The Workflow
RSS → Filter → Google Gemini → JSON Parse → Google Sheets
  • Built the workflow in Make.com instead of collecting information manually, using ChatGPT and Gemini to help design it
  • An RSS module watches selected sources for new items; a filter keeps only what's relevant by time or other conditions
  • Gemini summarises each item, extracts key points and classifies the topic or assesses the viewpoint using a preset prompt — returning structured JSON
  • A JSON parser maps title, summary, keywords and time, and Google Sheets adds each result as a new row automatically
✓ Less manual collection and copy-paste work
✓ Structured, searchable research records in Google Sheets
✓ Consistent summaries and topic tags across markets
✓ A practical example of applying AI to everyday operations
