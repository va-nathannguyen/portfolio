2.1 Hospitality & Retail · 2017–2020

Building better customer experiences in hospitality and retail

Situation

I worked in two customer-facing businesses at very different stages of development.

The first was a fine-dining restaurant in Hoi An that had been operating for less than a month when I joined. Service procedures were still largely undeveloped, and many employees had little or no restaurant experience. The business already had Facebook, Google Maps and TripAdvisor listings, but very little online visibility or customer feedback.

Later, I joined a multi-location art gallery business in Hanoi. Although it had an established physical presence, its online visibility was limited. Customer reviews were scarce or mixed, and the business had little engagement across its digital channels.

What I did

At the restaurant

  • Developed and introduced more structured service procedures.
  • Trained employees on the menu, fine-dining standards and how to interact with guests.
  • Worked directly with customers, adapting recommendations and service to individual preferences.
  • Encouraged guests to share reviews and trained staff to request customer feedback.

At the art gallery

  • Improved the customer journey from initial consultation and quotation through payment, hotel delivery and international shipping.
  • Recruited, trained and coordinated a 10-person team.
  • Created and developed Google Maps listings for gallery locations.
  • Photographed artworks to build a digital inventory and support online presentation.
  • Encouraged customer feedback and reviews to strengthen the business's online presence.
  • Proposed and implemented a remote-sales approach during COVID restrictions.
Results

Restaurant

Reached #1 among restaurants in Hoi An on TripAdvisor, with improvements to service standards, staff training and customer engagement contributing to its development.

Art gallery

Reached #1 in the Art Gallery category in Hanoi on Google Maps.

Improvements to service and business operations contributed to 20% sales growth and more than 200 five-star customer reviews.

The remote-sales approach generated tens of thousands of US dollars in revenue while in-person selling was heavily restricted.

2.2 Production Operations · 2024–2025

Coordinating high-volume operations through an unexpected system outage

Situation

As a team leader in high-end real-estate photo production, I coordinated daily workflows for international clients under strict deadlines and quality requirements.

Each shift involved approximately 60 people, including onsite employees, remote employees and freelance part-time editors.

The work required constant adjustments to priorities, resources and delivery schedules, especially when urgent or complex requests arrived.

What I did

Daily workflow coordination

  • Reviewed workloads, shift handovers, urgent requests and upcoming deadlines.
  • Assigned or reallocated work based on urgency, complexity, individual skills, capacity and availability.
  • Considered both onsite and remote staff for urgent or complex tasks when their skills and capacity were suitable.
  • Worked closely with Customer Success to clarify requirements, communicate realistic ETAs and keep clients informed through the customer-facing team.
  • Monitored progress and adjusted resources when delays or delivery risks appeared.

Handling an unexpected system outage

When the internal production system failed during urgent work, I helped coordinate an alternative way to keep operations running.

We switched to external communication channels, arranged for editors to continue working offline, used cloud storage for file delivery and relied on direct calls where necessary to keep everyone aligned.

Results
  • Urgent production work continued despite the system disruption.
  • Clearer ETA communication helped Customer Success manage client expectations.
  • Standardised SOPs and shift handovers supported more consistent execution.
  • The experience reinforced the importance of practical problem-solving, clear ownership and communication under pressure.
2.3 Software Sales & Support · 2021–2022

Helping non-technical customers use software

Situation

I worked with software designed for social-media marketing and account management. Customers included online-shop owners, livestream sellers and small businesses using platforms such as Facebook, TikTok and Zalo.

Many customers needed practical guidance to understand the software and use its features effectively.

What I did
  • Supported customers from initial enquiries through software installation, onboarding and user training.
  • Explained product features and technical steps in language that non-technical users could understand.
  • Asked questions to identify the cause of setup or usage issues.
  • Resolved problems within my scope and coordinated with technical colleagues when further investigation was required.
  • Followed up on customer questions and post-sale requests to keep issues moving towards resolution.
What I learned

Good customer support isn't just about knowing the product. It's also about understanding what the customer is trying to achieve, identifying what's stopping them, and explaining the next steps clearly.

When a problem requires specialist help, providing accurate context and following up can make a real difference to the customer's experience.

04

Approach

How I approach work, whether it's a customer issue, an urgent delivery or an open-ended request from a founder.

A

Understand before acting

I try to understand the situation before deciding what to do. That means asking questions, clarifying anything ambiguous and identifying what actually needs attention.

For urgent or complicated issues, a quick conversation can often be more effective than a long message thread.

B

Prioritise what matters

When several requests come in at once, I weigh urgency, impact, deadlines and available resources.

I also consider who has the right skills and capacity to handle each task, rather than simply following a fixed way of assigning work.

I try to set realistic expectations and communicate early when priorities or timelines need to change.

C

Take ownership and follow through

I believe in keeping responsibilities, progress and next steps clear.

When I can solve something within my scope, I work towards resolving it. When specialist help is needed, I provide the relevant context, coordinate with the right people and follow up where necessary.

I don't consider a problem properly handled just because it has been passed to someone else.

D

Keep improving how work gets done

I naturally pay attention to repetitive tasks, unclear procedures and avoidable problems.

I enjoy finding practical ways to simplify workflows, reduce manual effort and improve consistency without compromising quality.

I don't expect to know everything from the beginning. When I encounter something unfamiliar, I explore the options, learn what I need and work towards a solution.
